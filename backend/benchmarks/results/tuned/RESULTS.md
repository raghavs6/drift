---

> **`catalog` numbers here are cache-served and say nothing about read-path latency.**
> Step 14 put a 60s TTL in front of `/api/experiences`. The benchmark requests one
> constant filter for ~8 minutes, so after the first request nearly every hit is a
> dict lookup. Its p50 of 1-3 ms measures *not doing the work*, not doing it faster.
> `prefs`, `swipes` and `collections` still reach Postgres on every request and are
> the only remaining read-path signal.
>
> **The authed endpoints improved at 200 VUs as a side effect of the cache, not
> because the read path got faster.** p50 1.01s -> 164ms and p95 2.03s -> 574ms
> against `step13-async-readpath`. Encoding 1.6 MB was competing for the same CPU as
> every other request; removing it gave that CPU back.
>
> **Cache stampede, visible in `max` and nowhere else.** The miss path has no lock,
> so when the TTL expires every request arriving before the first one finishes
> re-encoding also misses and re-encodes. The signature is a median-to-max gap that
> widens with concurrency:
>
> | catalog | p50 | p99 | max |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 10 VUs | 1 ms | 2 ms | **315 ms** |
> | 50 VUs | 3 ms | 5 ms | **999 ms** |
> | 200 VUs | 3 ms | 6 ms | **4.19 s** |
>
> At 200 VUs that is a ~1400x spread between the median and the worst request. It
> stays out of p99 because only the requests unlucky enough to arrive during a
> refresh pay it — roughly 8 expiries across the run. Single-flight (one request
> recomputes while the rest wait on it, or are served the stale entry) would remove
> the cliff. Not done here: it is a real defect but a different change, and the
> numbers above should be on record before anything else moves.
