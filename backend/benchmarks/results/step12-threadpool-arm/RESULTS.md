> **What this arm is for.** Same tree as `step13-async-readpath` with 474fe77
> (the async read path) reverted, so the two differ by exactly one commit and the
> async-vs-threadpool comparison is controlled. This is steps 11+12.
>
> **Attribution, p50 at 50 VUs:**
>
> | endpoint | baseline | s11 | s11+12 (this) | s11+12+13 | step 12 | step 13 |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | `prefs` | 517 ms | 300 ms | 218 ms | 120 ms | −27.3% | **−45.0%** |
> | `swipes` | 545 ms | 304 ms | 220 ms | 122 ms | −27.6% | **−44.5%** |
> | `collections` | 534 ms | 298 ms | 217 ms | 121 ms | −27.2% | **−44.2%** |
> | `catalog` | 612 ms | 336 ms | 408 ms | 432 ms | **+21.4%** | +5.9% |
>
> **At 200 VUs the two arms differ qualitatively, not by a percentage:**
> baseline 95.2% failed, step 11 96.7%, this arm **97.7%** — and the async arm
> served the entire level (p50 ~1.0s, p95 ~2.0s). Pool sizing does not move the
> 200 VU ceiling at all; it is still the 40-token thread pool that binds, and
> only step 13 removes it.
>
> **This falsifies the prediction made before the run** that catalog p50 here
> would land at 300-360 ms and pin the catalog regression on step 13. It came in
> at 408 ms. Most of the catalog regression is step 12's (+72 ms), not step 13's
> (+24 ms). Enlarging the pool from 15 to 40 lets more requests encode ~1.5 MB of
> JSON concurrently, and that work is CPU-bound: past the CPU limit, more
> concurrency raises per-request latency while throughput plateaus. Consistent
> with catalog's *tail* improving as its median worsened (p99 784 ms → 576 ms).
